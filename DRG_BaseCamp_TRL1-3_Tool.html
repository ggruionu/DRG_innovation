Early Prototype Diagnostic
TRL 1–3

Perpetual optimization feels like progress. It is not.

A prototype that keeps changing has never been tested against reality. This diagnostic tells you whether you are genuinely ready to leave Base Camp — or whether you are still tinkering at altitude, burning oxygen.

01
Prototype Readiness
Is your design actually frozen?
Q1 · Design Freeze
How would you describe the current state of your prototype design?
Be honest. "Almost done" is not frozen.
Q2 · Scope Control
In the last 60 days, have you added new features or capabilities not in your original design specification?
Scope creep is the enemy of Base Camp. New ideas belong in version 2.
Q3 · Version Discipline
How many distinct prototype versions have you built in the past 6 months?
One iterated version is progress. Five versions without user testing is avoidance.
02
User Requirements
Does it meet what actual users need — not what you think they need?
Q4 · Requirements Source
Where did your prototype's key performance requirements come from?
Requirements defined by you alone are hypotheses. Requirements defined by users are evidence.
Q5 · Requirements Documentation
Do you have a written document listing specific, measurable performance requirements your prototype must meet?
"It works well" is not a requirement. "Accuracy ≥ 92% on blinded test set" is a requirement.
Q6 · External Evaluation
Has your prototype been evaluated against requirements by someone other than yourself or your immediate team?
You cannot objectively evaluate your own invention. External evaluation is not optional.
03
Reproducibility
Can it work outside your lab, run by someone else?
Q7 · Independent Replication
Has your core finding or prototype function been replicated by someone outside your immediate lab or team?
If only you can make it work, you have a skill — not a product.
Q8 · Protocol Documentation
If handed your prototype and documentation tomorrow, could a qualified person achieve the same results without asking you questions?
Tacit knowledge is a translation barrier. Write it down.
Q9 · Real-World Conditions
Has your prototype been tested under conditions that simulate real-world use — not optimized lab conditions?
Lab performance and real-world performance are almost never the same. The gap always surprises.
04
Camp 1 Readiness
Funding, regulatory, IP — are you equipped for the next climb?
Q10 · Funding Pathway
Do you have a specific funding pathway identified — SBIR, STTR, angel, accelerator, or institutional grant?
Camp 1 requires oxygen. Funding is that oxygen. "I'll figure it out" is not a plan.
Q11 · Regulatory Awareness
Do you know which regulatory pathway your innovation will require — FDA 510(k), De Novo, PMA, exempt, or non-device?
Regulatory surprises at Camp 1 end more climbs than any other single factor.
Q12 · IP Status
What is the current status of intellectual property protection for your innovation?
Climbing without IP protection means you may be building someone else's mountain.

Answer all 12 questions honestly. The mountain does not grade on a curve.
